How a Calories Burned Calculator Actually Works: The MET Formula

Behind every reputable calories burned calculator sits one simple equation:

Calories burned = MET × body weight (kg) × duration (hours)

MET stands for Metabolic Equivalent of Task. One MET is the energy you burn sitting completely still — roughly 1 kilocalorie per kilogram of body weight per hour. Every activity is rated by how many times harder than resting it is. Brisk walking is about 4.3 METs, meaning it burns roughly 4.3 times what you'd burn sitting on the couch for the same time.

Here's a worked example. A 70 kg person runs at 6 mph (9.8 METs) for 30 minutes:

9.8 × 70 × 0.5 = 343 kcal

The same run for a 90 kg person burns 441 kcal — same effort, bigger engine, more fuel consumed. That weight-dependence is the single most important thing a calories burned calculator captures, and it's why two people doing the identical workout see different numbers on their fitness trackers.

What Are MET Values? Your Activity List, Decoded

MET values come from the Compendium of Physical Activities, a research project led by Dr. Barbara Ainsworth that has catalogued the energy cost of human movement since 1993. The 2024 edition — the source behind BurnBoard's database — covers hundreds of activities measured or estimated in laboratory and field studies. Each value is an average across study participants, which is why we label ours as estimates.

  • Strength: light lifting (3.5), vigorous lifting (6.0), bodyweight circuits (8.0), kettlebells, battle ropes and CrossFit-style training.
  • Water: every swim stroke rated separately — more on swimming below.
  • Outdoor: hiking (6.0), backpacking (7.0), rock climbing (7.5), skiing, snowboarding, gardening and even shoveling snow (6.0).
  • Home & daily: the hidden burn of real life — vigorous cleaning (4.0), cooking (2.5), childcare (3.0), office sitting (1.5), driving (2.0) and sleeping (0.95).

How Many Calories Does Swimming Burn?

It's one of the most-searched fitness questions in the world — "how many calories does swimming burn" — because the answer depends enormously on the stroke. Swimming is not one activity; it's half a dozen, and the MET spread proves it:

Notice that butterfly (13.8 METs) burns more than twice what leisurely swimming does — technique and intensity matter as much as the sport itself. Water also cools you efficiently, so swimmers often underestimate their effort; the MET numbers don't lie even when your skin feels cool. If you're choosing between strokes for calorie burn, faster freestyle and breaststroke give the best return for most recreational swimmers, while butterfly is the undisputed champion if you can sustain it.

How Accurate Is a Calories Burned Calculator?

Honest answer: it's a solid estimate, not a measurement. The MET formula is the accepted standard in exercise science, but real burn varies with factors the equation can't see:

  • Body composition: muscle burns more than fat at the same weight, so two 70 kg people can differ by 5–10%.
  • Fitness level: trained athletes are more efficient and may burn slightly fewer calories for the same task.
  • Age and sex: resting metabolic rate declines with age; formulas averaged across populations smooth over this.
  • Technique and terrain: running on sand, cycling into wind, or swimming with poor form all raise the real number above the table value.
  • Afterburn (EPOC): vigorous exercise elevates metabolism for hours afterward — a bonus the basic formula doesn't count.

Expect real-world results within about 10–20% of the calculator's number. For weight management, consistency of measurement matters more than absolute precision: if you log the same way every week, the trend is trustworthy even if any single number is approximate. And a note of caution — this tool is for general fitness information only and is not medical advice. If you have a heart condition, are pregnant, or are managing a medical condition, check with a professional before changing your exercise habits.
